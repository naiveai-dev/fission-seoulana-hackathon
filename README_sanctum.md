## **1. Introduction: LST and AI Research Funding Challenges**

AI research today faces a **funding gap and centralization problem**. Many promising ideas depend on grants from governments or corporations, which are limited, slow, and often guided by centralized interests. **Donors** who want to support AI breakthroughs typically have to donate capital outright, losing liquidity, while **researchers** navigate bureaucratic grant processes that may overlook innovative or high-risk ideas. This creates a mismatch: tremendous amounts of capital lie in crypto networks earning yield, while important AI projects remain underfunded.

**Liquid Staking Tokens (LST)** offer a novel solution by unlocking the value of staked assets. In Solana, users can stake SOL to secure the network and receive LSTs (like mSOL, JitoSOL, etc.) that **continue earning staking rewards while remaining liquid**. LSTs can be traded or used in DeFi, effectively turning otherwise locked staking rewards into accessible yield streams. With Solana’s liquid staking adoption rising, there is a large and growing pool of yield that could be repurposed for public goods.

Meanwhile, the **DeSci(Decentralized Science)** movement has highlighted the need for new funding models for research. The challenge is how to direct *continuous DeFi yields* to *AI research funding* in a decentralized, incentive-aligned way. Our project addresses this by bridging Solana’s LST yield ecosystem with AI research funding through a **community-governed platform**. We tackle key pain points:

- **Funding sustainability:** Instead of one-time donations, use yield generated from staked assets as a renewable funding source. This creates a **crypto endowment** where only rewards are spent, preserving principal.
- **Donor liquidity and incentive:** Contributors retain ownership of their staked assets (and can withdraw if needed), lowering the barrier to funding philanthropy. They effectively **donate only the staking rewards**, not their base capital.
- **Decentralized decision-making:** Rather than a closed committee selecting grants, the community evaluates proposals via an open market mechanism (futarchy). This taps collective intelligence and aligns funding with projects predicted to have the highest impact.
- **Researcher empowerment and accountability:** Researchers anywhere can propose projects and secure funding based on merit and community belief in their success. On-chain reputation (via Fission ID) can validate their track record, ensuring **trust** and recognition for contributors.

In summary, by combining **LST yield streams** with **AI funding needs**, the platform aims to democratize and scale support for AI research. The following sections detail how yield is captured through Sanctum’s Infinity Pool, how futarchy-based governance allocates those funds, and how a reputation system can bolster credibility – all contributing to an open, transparent AI research ecosystem.

## 2. **Yield Stream Design via Sanctum Infinity Pool**

**Sanctum Infinity Pool** is the core yield engine for our funding model. Infinity is a **multi-LST liquidity pool** on Solana containing dozens of staking derivatives of SOL. When users deposit SOL or any supported LST into the pool, they receive **INF tokens** as a receipt. **INF is a yield-bearing token**: over time, each INF increases in value as it **accrues staking rewards from the basket of LST plus swap fees** generated by liquidity provisioning. In practice, INF’s APY (Annual Percentage Yield) is composed of roughly the **average staking yield (~7%** on Solana) **+ DEX trading fees** from LST swaps. This diversified yield is robust – even if one validator’s APY is lower, trading activity on that LST can contribute fees to the pool’s overall return, smoothing out yield for INF holders.

Our platform’s **treasury** will leverage this by holding INF tokens and using the yield to fund research grants. The design works as follows:

1. **Donor Deposits:** Contributors deposit SOL (or existing LSTs) into a specialized **Treasury Vault** on our platform. Under the hood, the Vault stakes these assets into the Sanctum Infinity Pool and receives INF tokens. Donors could optionally receive a representation of their share (e.g., an NFT or a balance in the Vault) – maintaining the option to withdraw their principal later if needed. This ensures that **principal deposits remain intact and liquid**, while the **yield is directed to funding**.
2. **Yield Accrual:** As time passes, the INF held by the treasury grows in value. We track the **yield accumulation per epoch** (Solana epochs are ~2-3 days) or per a set period. For example, if 100 SOL worth of assets are deposited, after one month the INF might be redeemable for ~101.5 SOL (assuming ~1.5% yield in that period). That ~1.5 SOL profit represents **new funds available** for grants, generated purely from staking and fees.
3. **Yield Extraction:** At intervals, the smart contract **harvests the yield** while preserving principal. This can be done by redeeming a small portion of the INF back to SOL (or directly to a stablecoin) equal to the accrued rewards. For instance, continuing the example, the contract could withdraw 1.5 SOL worth, leaving the equivalent of 100 SOL still invested. The withdrawn yield is moved into a **Funding Pool** (denominated in a stable asset like USDC or SOL) ready to be allocated to approved research proposals. This **streaming of yield** can occur continuously or periodically (e.g., monthly), effectively turning staking rewards into a **steady funding stream** for grants.
4. **Distribution to Researchers:** When the community approves a grant (via governance described later), the required amount of yield (now in the Funding Pool) is disbursed to the researcher’s wallet. To mitigate volatility, yield can be converted to **USDC** at the moment of payout, giving researchers a stable amount for budgeting. The principal remains staked, continuing to generate future funding. Over time, as more donors join and stake, the **fund’s capacity grows cumulatively**.

By using Sanctum’s Infinity Pool, we ensure **maximal capital efficiency and decentralization** of the yield source. Infinity diversifies across many validators and LSTs, preventing over-reliance on any single staking provider. Donors benefit from one of the most liquid and high-yielding SOL staking avenues, while the platform gains a predictable **yield stream to fuel AI research**. Importantly, this design **aligns incentives**: donors keep their stake and even the INF token’s appreciating value (so they gain from Solana’s growth and base yield), and they simultaneously contribute to scientific progress through the yield – creating a win-win scenario that can attract more capital to Solana’s staking ecosystem and more funding to researchers.

## 3. **Futarchy Governance (Prediction Market-Based Decisions)**

Allocating funds effectively is as crucial as generating them. Rather than relying on centralized committees or simple token voting, our platform uses **futarchy governance** – a mechanism where **prediction markets guide decisions**. Futarchy (coined by economist Robin Hanson) suggests to “vote on values, but bet on beliefs,” meaning the community sets a goal (e.g., maximizing research success) and prediction markets determine which proposals best achieve that goal. On Solana, **MetaDAO** provides a live implementation of futarchy for DAO governance, making it an ideal choice for our platform’s decision-making process

### **How it works**

For each AI research proposal submitted, the platform spins up a pair of prediction markets representing the decision to **fund or not fund** that proposal. Specifically, with MetaDAO’s model, we would create two outcome markets:

- **Market A (“Fund Proposal X”):** Traders buy and sell tokens that payoff based on the scenario where the proposal *is funded*. The market price reflects the community’s belief in the value/impact of funding this project.
- **Market B (“Do Not Fund Proposal X”):** A parallel market where tokens payoff based on the proposal *not being funded* (or funds reserved for other uses). Its price indicates expected value if the project is rejected.

These markets are typically tied to a **measure of success** – in MetaDAO’s own DAO, they use the DAO’s token price as the metric. For our research grants, success could be quantified by a future metric (e.g. the project achieving a certain milestone, or a peer-reviewed publication output in a year). In practice, to keep it simple, we can treat the **platform’s governance token** or even a **dummy metric token** as the value marker that traders speculate on. The key is that **whichever scenario yields a higher expected value will signal the favorable decision**. After a set period of market trading, the outcome is determined: if “Fund” tokens trade at a higher price than “Not Fund” tokens, it indicates the crowd believes funding the project leads to better outcomes for the community’s goals. In that case, the proposal is approved for funding. If the opposite, it’s rejected.

**Example:** Suppose a proposal “Project Alpha – Open AI model for climate” is up for a 500 SOL grant. Two markets run for a week. In Market A, “If funded, Project Alpha will result in successful outcomes,” the price of the outcome token stabilizes at **$0.70** (70% implied confidence in success/benefit). In Market B, “If not funded,” the token is at **$0.40** (implying lower expected value if we don’t fund it). Because the “Fund” scenario has higher market-assessed value, the platform would approve funding for Project Alpha. Traders who believed in the project make profits by buying early at lower prices and selling higher, while skeptics who shorted “Fund” lose – thus **financially rewarding correct foresight** and penalizing poor predictions. This incentivizes participants to research proposals deeply and **only back those they truly think will succeed**.

### **MetaDAO Integration**

We leverage MetaDAO’s smart contracts to implement these markets. MetaDAO’s futarchy framework on Solana allows any project to set up proposal markets with a **“pass vs fail” structure and on-chain price feeds**. Notably, MetaDAO has already been used by others for grant decisions: the Solana project Drift used futarchy markets to decide token grants, finding that the community “is thinking very carefully around spend” and successfully accepted one proposal while rejecting another based on market outcomes. We will follow a similar approach:

- When a research proposal is submitted, our platform calls MetaDAO’s program to create a futarchy proposal with the necessary parameters (proposal ID, funding amount, evaluation deadline, etc.).
- The community (including SOL holders, donors, or even outside speculators) can then **connect their wallets and trade** in these markets. This could be done through an embedded MetaDAO interface or via our UI redirecting to MetaDAO’s trading portal for that specific proposal.
- The **governance token** of our platform (if we introduce one) or INF token value can serve as the metric that markets are predicting. For simplicity in a hackathon prototype, we might simulate a scalar outcome (0 or 1 for success) and have the market essentially function like a yes/no prediction market.
- Once the market period ends, the smart contract **compares the prices** (or implied probabilities). If “Fund” > “Not Fund” by the close, the proposal is marked as approved. The allocated yield (from the Funding Pool) is then **unlocked to the researcher**.
- Over the longer term, when the proposal’s outcome is determined (e.g., the researcher delivers a promised result or hits a milestone), an **oracle** or verification process will resolve the markets. If the project indeed succeeded (however success is defined for that proposal), then the “Fund” tokens pay out 1:1 and “Not Fund” pay out 0 (or vice versa if it failed). This lets those who bet correctly claim rewards, completing the futarchy cycle. For initial implementation, this resolution could even be manual or based on community attestation due to time constraints, but the framework supports eventual automation.

### **Advantages of Futarchy for AI funding**

- It **leverages collective intelligence**: People with knowledge in the field (scientists, engineers, investors) can back their assessments with real stakes. If a proposal is truly promising, more informed traders will drive its “Fund” price up; if it’s unsound, the market will reflect doubts.
- It reduces bias and plutocracy compared to 1-token-1-vote. Even a small stakeholder can have big influence by betting aggressively on a proposal they believe in, and they’ll earn profit for being right. This encourages merit-based outcomes rather than popularity contests.
- It creates a **feedback mechanism**: The market prices give real-time insight into how a proposal is perceived. Researchers could even watch their proposal’s market to gauge community sentiment and address concerns (though they cannot directly “campaign” with tokens unless they want to bet on themselves).
- It inherently ties funding to expected **impact** (as defined by the metric). Over time, if the platform’s token value is used as the metric, it means the community expects funding good projects to increase the platform’s value (e.g., maybe through reputational value or potential IP – though our focus is on open research, a token could still capture the ecosystem’s success).

Overall, using futarchy via MetaDAO adds a layer of **economic rationality and transparency** to grant allocation. Decisions are made in an open market that anyone can observe or participate in, and every funded project comes with a **quantified confidence level from the community**. This innovative governance model sets our platform apart and aligns with the **open, experimental spirit** of both Web3 and scientific research.

## 4. **Decentralized Reputation: Fission ID Integration**

While capital allocation is important, **credibility and accountability** of researchers and evaluators are equally crucial in a grant system. To enhance trust, we propose integrating **Fission ID** – a decentralized identity and reputation protocol on Solana – as an **opt-in reputation layer** for the platform’s users. Fission ID provides each participant (especially researchers) with a NFT that represents their identity and reputation, updating in real-time based on verified contributions. This can serve as a kind of on-chain **ORCID (Open Researcher ID)**, linking a scientist’s outputs to a permanent profile.

In practice, here’s how Fission ID would augment our platform:

### **Researcher Profile and Verification**

When a researcher signs up to submit a proposal, they can attach a Fission ID or mint a new one through our platform’s interface. This NFT could contain basic verified info (for example, it could be attested with their real identity or pseudonymous profile, academic credentials, or existing ORCID publications if linked). If a researcher has prior contributions (papers, open-source code, datasets) that have been validated in the Fission ID system, their NFT metadata will reflect a **trust score or badges** summarizing their track record.

### **Contribution Logging**

As projects are funded and executed, their **outcomes are recorded** to researchers’ Fission IDs. For example, if a funded project results in a published paper or a released dataset, a hash or identifier of that output can be added on-chain (perhaps via a reference in the grant contract or by community attestation). Fission ID’s protocol, which uses an AI-assisted validation system, can verify the authenticity/quality of the contribution (e.g., confirm the paper is published in a reputable journal, or the code meets certain standards) and then **update the NFT’s metadata** with this achievement. The NFT could gain an attribute like “Published 1 peer-reviewed paper via Platform X grant” or increase the researcher’s credibility score. This **permanent, public log of contributions** builds a verifiable resume for the researcher on-chain.

### **Reputation-Weighted Influence**

Optionally, the platform’s governance can leverage reputation scores. For instance, community members who are making prediction market bets or voting on parameter changes could have their influence moderated by their Fission ID reputation (to mitigate sybil attacks or amplify expert voices). While our primary governance is market-based (futarchy), reputation could play a role in other areas, such as **peer review of proposal milestones** or flagging dishonest behavior. A researcher or evaluator with a history of accurate assessments and contributions would naturally carry more weight. This creates a **meritocratic ecosystem** beyond just financial stakes.

### **Data Labeling and Curation**

Many AI research projects involve creating or curating datasets. We can involve the community in these tasks (for example, labeling data or evaluating model outputs) and use Fission ID to **track their contribution quality**. Fission’s framework includes an **Enhanced Data Labeling & Validation** component where users gain reputation for accurate labeling and reviewing. If our funded project opens up tasks to the public (a bit like a decentralized Mechanical Turk for AI), participants could earn improvements to their Fission ID by contributing high-quality work, as verified by consensus or AI checks. This in turn could qualify them for rewards or for involvement in more complex evaluations (much like a community of vetted annotators). For our platform, this means **better quality control** on any community-sourced work and an incentive for community engagement beyond just funding and betting.

Integrating Fission ID brings **trust and transparency** to the human side of the platform:

- Researchers cannot easily fake credentials or results – their Fission ID will reflect only *validated* outputs, and it is **continuously updated** and publicly inspectable.
- Funders and community members can check a researcher’s Fission ID profile to see if they have a strong track record (e.g., “This person has successfully delivered 3 projects in the past and has high peer-review scores”). This aids in decision-making on proposals.
- It creates a **long-term incentive** for researchers to deliver quality work: a successful project boosts their on-chain reputation, which could help them secure future funding (on this platform or elsewhere in DeSci). Conversely, failing to deliver could be noted (or simply not improve their score), discouraging misuse of funds.
- Over time, a rich **reputation network** forms, connecting researchers, reviewers, and contributors. This could enable further features, such as reputational rewards, targeted bounties for high-rep experts, or filtering proposals (for example, giving established contributors fast-track consideration).

While Fission ID integration is optional and may not be fully implemented in the hackathon prototype, we have designed the system with **hooks for identity verification**. For instance, our proposal submission form can include a field for Fission ID, and our backend can interact with the Fission ID program on Solana to fetch and display reputation data. In the long run, this partnership would turn our funding platform into a part of a broader **DeSci ecosystem**, where funding, execution, and validation of research are all **on-chain and interconnected**. As the Fission ID team envisions, this approach can solve trust issues in Web3 and science by providing a **“living” reputation that continuously evolves with one’s contributions**. We believe this aligns perfectly with our goal of advancing AI research in an open and accountable manner.

### Technical Feasibility and Prototype Implementation

From a technical standpoint, the project is ambitious but **feasible with Solana’s current stack**. We outline the architecture and prototype implementation, demonstrating how each piece can be built or simulated:

### **Solana Program (On-Chain Logic)**

At the heart is a custom Solana program (written in Rust, e.g., using the Anchor framework) that manages deposits, yield harvesting, and proposal state. Key functions include:

- **Deposit & Withdrawal:** Accept SOL (or LST) deposits from users into a **Treasury Vault account**. The program interacts with Sanctum’s **Stake Pool program** to deposit these funds into Infinity Pool (by calling the CPI into Sanctum’s contract or via an SDK). The returned INF tokens are held by the program (in its token account) on behalf of the depositor. If a user withdraws, the program similarly converts the requisite INF back to SOL.
- **Yield Accounting:** Keep track of the INF token amount vs. its initial deposit value. Because INF value appreciates, the program can compute yield by comparing current INF exchange rate or by periodically querying the INF-SOL conversion (Sanctum likely provides an oracle or we derive from pool data). A simpler approach used in our prototype is to assume a fixed APY and increment a “yield counter.” For hack demo purposes, we simulated yield by incrementing a balance each epoch. In production, the program could call a **price/oracle feed** for INF or measure directly by attempting a tiny redemption to calculate growth.
- **Funding Pool & Distribution:** The program holds a **Funding Pool account** (denominated in SOL or USDC). When a proposal is approved, the specified amount is transferred from this pool to the researcher’s address. Replenishing the pool happens via yield harvesting: our program can trigger a partial withdrawal of INF (only the accrued portion) and move those funds to the Funding Pool. This might be done by an authorized bot or a cron-like scheduler off-chain calling an instruction every epoch.
- **Proposal Creation:** We maintain a registry of proposals (each with data: proposer, description hash, requested amount, etc.). When a proposal is added, the program could also store references to the futarchy market (e.g., an ID or address from MetaDAO’s program) and a status (pending, approved, rejected, funded).
- **State Transitions:** After the futarchy decision period, a callback or a manual trigger updates the proposal status on our program. If approved, the funds move; if rejected, funds stay in the pool. This state is important to prevent double spends and to allow the front-end to query the list of funded projects.

### **Integration with Sanctum Infinity**

Sanctum provides a **deployed program** for the Infinity Pool and an interface for deposits. Since Infinity supports deposits of SOL directly for INF, our implementation uses that: the treasury program transfers incoming SOL to the Sanctum program (with proper approval and accounts) and receives INF. During the hackathon, if direct integration was complex, we **mocked this interaction** by creating a dummy INF token in our local environment that increases in value. However, given Sanctum’s open documentation and perhaps existing SDK, it’s feasible to integrate directly. (The **Infinity pool’s program ID and instructions** are known from Sanctum docs/audits, and we would use Solana’s CPI to invoke it.) Security is paramount – we ensure only our treasury program can control the INF tokens and that donor deposits are tracked in a map of user -> INF amount for withdrawal logic.

### **MetaDAO Futarchy Markets**

We do not need to reinvent prediction markets; instead, we piggyback on MetaDAO’s protocol:

- MetaDAO likely provides an **API or SDK** for creating proposals. For the prototype, we **simulated the futarchy** outcome by allowing users to input or randomly generate a “market result” for a proposal (due to time constraints). However, our design anticipates using MetaDAO’s on-chain program. This means when a proposal is submitted on our platform, we call MetaDAO’s program (perhaps via their frontend or through a direct Solana instruction if open). MetaDAO would then handle the trading logic in its own contract.
- For integration, one approach is to treat our platform as a **user of MetaDAO**: e.g., create an org or sub-org on MetaDAO for our platform and then each proposal is a futarchy proposal under that org. Their docs and recent hackathon usage suggest this is doable. We’d store the MetaDAO proposal ID in our proposal data for reference.
- The **market UI** can be embedded or linked. In the prototype UI, clicking a proposal’s “Prediction Market” button could open MetaDAO’s webapp to the specific market page (since MetaDAO has a web interface for trading). For a seamless UX, a future iteration might integrate via an iframe or an SDK to show the order book and allow trades directly on our site.
- After the market is closed, **settlement data** (which scenario won) can be fetched from MetaDAO’s contract state. We can implement an off-chain agent or on-chain logic (if MetaDAO provides a callback) to relay the winning outcome to our program, which then triggers funding distribution automatically.

### **Fission ID**

Fission ID’s system consists of **smart contracts for minting and updating NFTs**. We plan to integrate at the application layer:

- During user onboarding (for researchers or heavy participants), our front-end can prompt them to link an existing Fission ID NFT or mint a new one. Minting might call Fission’s contract to create a **NFT** in their wallet. Alternatively, we can simply note their Fission ID and use off-chain queries to display their stats.
- Updating the NFT with new achievements likely requires either the Fission backend/AI validation to signal our contract, or our platform to issue an attestation. In a prototype, we won’t implement the full AI validation loop, but we will simulate by manually updating a dummy profile when a milestone is reached.
- We ensure all **research outcome data** (like links to papers, etc.) are logged either in our contract or off-chain DB, so they can be fed into Fission’s validation later. Technical feasibility here is high because it’s mostly about **data interoperability** and less about heavy logic on our side.

### **Front-End (UI/UX)**

We will be building a web application (using React and Solana’s wallet adapter) to provide a polished user experience:

- **Dashboard:** shows total staked amount, current yield rate, and available funding pool size (all fetched from on-chain accounts). It updates in near real-time using Solana RPC calls or WebSocket subscriptions to our program’s data.
- **Staking Interface:** allows a user to connect their Phantom (for example) and deposit SOL. It shows their contribution, accrued yield (simulated), and an option to withdraw. We emphasize UX here – clear indication that their principal is safe and how much “impact” their yield has generated.
- **Proposal Submission:** a form for researchers to input proposal details (title, description, funding requested, outcome metrics, etc.). We likely store full descriptions off-chain (IPFS or Arweave) and keep only a hash/reference on-chain to save space. Once submitted, the proposal appears in a list with a status “Voting ongoing” and a link to its futarchy market.
- **Proposal List & Markets:** each proposal card shows key info (researcher name or pseudonym, requested amount, maybe their Fission ID trust score, and the **current market odds** for funding). For example, it might show “Fund likelihood: 68%” based on current prediction market prices, which we compute by querying the MetaDAO market. Users can click in to see more and participate (leading them to MetaDAO trading UI or an integrated form to place a bet by signing a tx).
- **Governance Panel:** for transparency, we include a section where all decision data is visible – e.g., a record of past proposals, their market outcomes, and whether they were funded. This acts like an archive or ledger of all grants given.
- **Profile/Identity:** if using Fission ID, a researcher or contributor can have a profile page showing their SBT stats: e.g., reputation score, badges (like “AI Domain Expert” or “Top 10% predictor”), and linked outputs. This pulls data from the Fission ID contract (which we can query via Solana RPC or an indexer). Even without deep integration, we can manually display known info (like number of publications, etc., if provided).

In summary, our prototype demonstrates that the concept is technically achievable by combining existing Solana DeFi infrastructure with some new glue code. **Solana’s high throughput and low fees** are critical – they allow micro-distributions of yield and rapid market transactions without prohibitive cost, and Fission ID can update frequently thanks to cheap transactions. We are confident that with further development, this platform can be launched on mainnet, given the foundation built during the hackathon.

## 5. Impact and Innovation

Our project’s impact lies at the intersection of decentralized finance, governance innovation, and scientific progress. By design, it embodies **decentralization, transparency, and the open advancement of AI research**. Here we highlight the key impacts and innovative aspects:

### **🌐 Democratizing AI Research Funding**

This platform enables a global community of SOL holders to collectively fund AI initiatives they believe in. It **lowers the barrier** for participation in science funding – anyone with even a small stake can contribute simply by staking and/or by participating in proposal markets. This contrasts with traditional grant systems that are limited to governments or wealthy patrons. The community-driven approach means research directions are decided by the many, not the few, potentially diversifying the kinds of AI projects that get supported (including high-risk, high-reward ideas or niche innovations that might be overlooked by mainstream institutions).

### **♻️ Sustainable, Principal-Protected Funding**

The use of staking yields as opposed to one-time donations is highly innovative in philanthropy. It creates a **perpetual endowment model** where the fund can exist indefinitely, supporting new projects continuously. Donors effectively **“donate without loss,”** giving them strong incentive to contribute more capital. This could unlock **significant new funding for AI** – for example, if even a small fraction of Solana stakers allocate their yield, it could mean millions of dollars yearly towards open AI research. Such a model is **scalable**: as Solana and liquid staking grow, the funding pool grows with it, in a virtuous cycle that also strengthens Solana by attracting more staking (a positive feedback loop for both the network and the research sector).

### **🔮 Meritocratic Governance via Futarchy**

Using prediction markets for governance is cutting-edge. It brings **market efficiency and accountability** to decision-making. This is a clear innovation over token voting or committee decisions, mitigating issues like voter apathy, whale dominance, or nepotism. Every decision is backed by rationale expressed in market prices, which are harder to game without financial risk. For the AI field, this means **smarter allocation of resources** – projects that truly show promise (in the eyes of knowledgeable peers and investors) are the ones that get funded. Over time, if the markets prove accurate, more trust will be placed in the system’s ability to pick winners, potentially attracting even **traditional funding sources** to collaborate (imagine governments or NGOs observing our markets as signals of what research is worth funding). Moreover, futarchy’s success here could serve as a model for other DeSci and DAO funding endeavors, **pioneering a new governance paradigm** in the scientific community.

### **🔗 Enhanced Trust and Transparency with On-Chain Reputation**

By integrating Fission ID and keeping records on-chain, we ensure **unprecedented transparency** in research funding. All proposals, decisions, and outcomes are publicly viewable. Stakeholders can track how money was used and what it achieved. Researchers build verifiable reputations, which **incentivizes honesty and hard work**. Instances of fraud or non-delivery are less likely (or can be quickly exposed), addressing a common concern in grant funding about accountability. Fission ID approach means that **outcomes directly affect reputation** – a level of accountability traditional systems lack. This transparency can also foster **collaboration**: researchers can find each other via their on-chain profiles and maybe team up (e.g., a well-regarded AI ethicist sees another’s project and joins as an advisor). The **community labeling/validation** aspect (if implemented) means even the data and results get community verification, leading to higher quality outputs (and concurrently, a knowledgeable community that deeply engages with the research).

### **🤝 Fostering Open Science and Global Access**

We intend for all research outputs funded through the platform to be **open-source or published openly**. Since the community is funding it, the results belong to the community (and humanity) rather than being patented or paywalled. This **open AI advancement** ethos ensures that breakthroughs – whether code, models, or findings – can be used, replicated, and built upon by others, accelerating the field. Additionally, the platform does not discriminate by geography or credentials; a young researcher in a developing country has as much opportunity to propose and get funded as a professor at a top university, provided they can convince the community of their idea. This could **unlock a wealth of global talent** and ideas, truly decentralizing who gets to innovate in AI.

### **💡 Innovation Synergy (DeFi meets DeSci)**

The combination of **DeFi yield protocols + DAO governance + DeSci reputation** is novel. Each component (liquid staking, futarchy, on-chain ID) is emerging on its own, but our project is among the first to **synthesize them into a cohesive application**. This showcases the composability of Web3: we’re effectively creating a pipeline where staked tokens flow into scientific output, guided by market-based collective intelligence and verified by decentralized identity. Such a holistic approach is rare and positions the project at the forefront of Web3 innovation. It demonstrates a creative use of Solana’s capabilities (high-speed finance and data storage for social impact) that could inspire other cross-domain applications (e.g., using DeFi yields for climate projects, using futarchy to govern public goods allocations, etc.).

### **📈 Market Potential and Ecosystem Growth**
