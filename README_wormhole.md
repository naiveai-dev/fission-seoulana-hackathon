## 1. **Introduction**

Cross-chain **Futarchy governance** is a novel model that uses prediction markets to drive DAO decisions instead of direct voting. In Futarchy, stakeholders *“vote on values but bet on beliefs”* – the community agrees on success metrics (values) and then bets on which proposal will better achieve those metrics. The result is a governance system where market dynamics inform which policies are adopted, leveraging collective intelligence for more informed decisions.

This document proposes a **technical architecture for cross-chain Futarchy governance** targeting Solana as the hub chain. It integrates **Wormhole** bridge technology to allow governance token holders from multiple chains (e.g. Ethereum, Polygon) to participate on Solana, and **Fission ID**’s decentralized identity framework to link user identities across chains with reputation-based weighting. The goal is a feasible, innovative model enabling a unified Futarchy governance process across networks, with Solana providing the high-performance execution environment.

We outline how governance tokens from external chains can be securely bridged into Solana via Wormhole and used in prediction market contracts. We also detail how Fission ID enables cross-chain identity linkage and reputation scoring, so that participants’ contributions and credibility (from any chain) factor into governance. Special focus is given to Solana’s technical role – its throughput, low latency, and smart contract capabilities – in orchestrating the prediction markets and governance logic. A system architecture diagram and example use-cases (in DeSci and multi-chain DeFi governance) illustrate the end-to-end process from identity linking and token bridging to prediction market resolution and cross-chain execution.

## 2. Background

### 2-1. **Futarchy Governance Model**

**Futarchy** (proposed by economist Robin Hanson) is a governance model where decisions are determined by prediction markets rather than direct votes. In a Futarchy-based DAO, a proposal’s outcome is chosen based on market prices that predict a success metric if the proposal is adopted vs. if it isn’t. In essence, **participants bet on outcomes**: if they believe a policy will improve the chosen metric, they buy shares in the market for that policy’s success; if not, they back the alternative. Market prices (driven by real-money bets) reflect the collective expectation of which decision yields a better result. Once the betting period ends, the **decision with the higher implied success probability “wins”** and is enacted. Later, an oracle evaluates the actual metric, and bettors who chose correctly are rewarded, incentivizing honest, informed predictions. This approach aims to harness wisdom of crowds and expert insight, mitigating uninformed voting.

Key requirements for Futarchy include clearly defined proposals and metrics, complete outcome sets (e.g. *Policy A* vs *Policy B*), a trusted oracle for measuring the result, and a liquid prediction market for trading outcome tokens. Historically, Futarchy has been experimental but it shows promise for DAO governance. On Solana, Futarchy can be especially powerful due to fast, cheap transactions enabling real-time market-driven governance. This model does introduce complexity (defining metrics, preventing manipulation), but combining it with identity and cross-chain reach can improve its robustness.

### 2-2. **Fission ID for Decentralized Identity & Reputation**

**Fission ID** is a decentralized identity and reputation framework that creates a **unified on-chain identity** for users, represented as a NFT on Solana. And this NFT a user’s reputation score, achievements, and credentials are **continuously tracked and written to their NFT identity**. For example, contributions to decentralized science (DeSci) research, successful DAO proposals, or accurate prediction market bets could all incrementally raise a user’s trust score on their Fission ID. 

Fission ID’s system involves off-chain data validation (using AI and community feedback) and on-chain updates. It leverages Solana’s high throughput and low fees to support frequent updates to reputation data. In practice, **Fission ID allows linking a single user’s identities across multiple chains and contexts** – acting as an **“on-chain ORCID”** that can permanently link contributions (publications, DAO participation, etc.) to one verifiable profile. Users might, for instance, link their Ethereum wallet and Solana wallet to the same Fission ID NFT, unifying their identity. This linkage can be done by cryptographic verification (e.g. signing a message with the Ethereum address and recording it in the Solana identity registry), enabling **cross-chain identity mapping**.

In governance, Fission ID introduces a **reputation-weighted component**. Traditional token voting (1 token = 1 vote) is prone to Sybil attacks (one entity using multiple addresses) and plutocracy (rich holders dominating). By incorporating a user’s Fission ID reputation score, the system can **give more weight or privileges to users who have demonstrated expertise and commitment**. For example, governance **voting power or proposal rights can be influenced by Fission ID score** – a member with a history of valuable contributions (tracked on-chain) might have proportionally greater influence than a new or inactive member, even if pure token count is similar. This **ensures informed, community-aligned decisions**, as those with proven track records have stronger voice, mitigating purely wealth-based governance.

In the Futarchy model, Fission ID can play multiple roles:

- **Identity Linking:** Only users with a verified Fission ID (i.e., who have linked their wallets and perhaps passed certain checks) can participate in the governance markets. This makes it harder to spin up fake accounts to manipulate outcomes, since each identity is singular and tied to reputation.
- **Reputation-Weighted Participation:** The **outcome of prediction markets could incorporate reputation** by, say, requiring less collateral from high-rep users for the same influence, or by separately tracking the “reputation-weighted opinion” of the market. As a simple example, a proposal might require that at least some high-reputation participants are betting in its favor to be considered valid, adding a qualitative gate.
- **Dynamic Reputation Feedback:** Because Futarchy rewards accurate predictions, **users who consistently bet on successful outcomes will earn profits – which can be fed into their Fission ID as reputation gain**. The system could log a successful governance prediction as a contribution, increasing that identity’s trust score. Conversely, those repeatedly betting on bad outcomes might see a dip in their reputation. Over time, this creates feedback: proven “forecasters” gain more influence, aligning incentives for honest participation. In essence, **Fission ID provides a memory of users’ governance performance**.

By combining Wormhole and Fission ID, we get a **cross-chain identity and reputation system**: no matter which chain a user’s tokens or actions originate from, they can be attributed to the **same person** on Solana. This is foundational for a fair cross-chain Futarchy – it enables **one-person-one-identity** (if not one-vote) and **trust propagation** across networks, enriching the purely market-based futarchy with a layer of accountable identity.

## 3. **Architecture and System Design**

![image](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/e9b746e7-a91c-47b3-9cee-ba9ff974a0b0)


*High-level cross-chain governance architecture (hub-and-spoke model). Solana acts as the **Hub Chain** coordinating proposals and prediction markets, while external networks like Ethereum and Polygon serve as **Spoke Chains** connected via Wormhole. Governance tokens flow from spokes to the hub, and governance decisions (if needed) can flow back to spokes via cross-chain messages. This design enables a unified Futarchy process across multiple blockchains.*

### System Overview: Hub and Spokes

The **governance system uses a hub-and-spoke architecture**: **Solana is the Hub** where all core governance logic resides, and other chains (Ethereum, Polygon, etc.) are Spokes that interface through Wormhole. The Hub chain (Solana) hosts the **Futarchy governance program**, the prediction market contracts, and the Fission ID identity registry. Spoke chains host the original governance tokens and local contracts that can send/receive messages via Wormhole but do not run the governance logic themselves.

In this model, Solana as the hub acts as the **central controller** for proposal creation, market execution, and resolution, while the spoke chains are mainly sources of voters (token holders) and destinations for executing the decided outcome (if the outcome affects those chains). Wormhole’s infrastructure links them by carrying tokens and oracle messages across chain boundaries. The sequence below describes the **full cross-chain participation process** from start to finish:

1. **Identity Linkage (Pre-requisite):** A user first creates or claims their **Fission ID on Solana**. This involves minting the Fission ID NBT (if not already minted) and linking any external addresses. For example, an Ethereum governance token holder would sign a verification message with their Ethereum wallet and submit it to the Fission ID program on Solana, which records the Ethereum address in the user’s identity profile. Now the system knows that Ethereum address **X** and Solana address **Y** belong to the *same individual*. The user’s Fission ID NFT metadata is updated to reflect any reputation or credentials from that external account (such as past DAO participation, contributions, etc.). This step ensures **cross-chain identity coherence** – one person equals one on-chain identity – before any governance action is taken.
2. **Token Bridging to Solana:** When a governance proposal is announced, token holders on external chains prepare to participate in the Solana-based futarchy market. Using the Wormhole **Portal Token Bridge**, a user **bridges their governance tokens from the source chain to Solana**. For instance, a user with 100 governance tokens on Ethereum will send those tokens to the Wormhole contract on Ethereum, specifying their Solana address (the one linked to their Fission ID) as the recipient. **Wormhole guardians attest** the lockup on Ethereum and the Solana contract mints **100 wrapped governance tokens** (SPL tokens representing the Ethereum asset) to the user’s Solana wallet. This process can be done similarly from Polygon or any supported chain – Wormhole’s network supports all major chains, enabling tokens from various ecosystems to co-exist on Solana. Once bridged, the user’s tokens are now in the Solana environment, ready to be used in smart contracts. (Note: If the governance tokens already exist natively on Solana as well, those can be used directly alongside bridged ones – all participants use the Solana-based tokens for the governance market.)
3. **Proposal Creation on Solana:** A new governance proposal is formally created on the Solana hub. This could be initiated through a **Governance Hub Program** (similar to SPL Governance or a custom Futarchy Governor program). The proposal details include:
    - **Policy options**: e.g. *Proposal A (adopt change)* vs *Proposal B (status quo)*.
    - **Success Metric**: e.g. “protocol revenue in next quarter” or any quantifiable KPI that the DAO cares about.
    - **Market parameters**: betting period duration, market currency, etc.
    - **Oracle specification**: how the success metric will be measured (e.g. which oracle or calculation, and at what future date).Once created, the proposal is announced on-chain (and could be mirrored to other chains via Wormhole messages if needed for transparency). Because identity is integrated, the system could require that a **proposal sponsor** have a minimum Fission ID reputation score (ensuring only credible members can open a Futarchy market for a proposal, preventing spam). This is an example of reputation-weighted access control in action.
4. **Prediction Market Initialization:** Upon proposal creation, the **Futarchy smart contract** on Solana sets up a **prediction market** for that proposal. This typically means creating two sets of **outcome tokens**: one that pays out if Proposal A is implemented *and* the success metric later validates (often called *A_outcome token*), and one for Proposal B (the alternative). Initially, these outcome tokens can be created in equal measure (for a complete set) and perhaps an automated market maker (AMM) or orderbook is established so that they can be traded. For example, 1 governance token’s worth might be redeemable for either 1 *A_outcome* + 1 *B_outcome* (representing a complete set covering both outcomes). The prediction market contract might use an AMM to price these outcome tokens such that their combined price equals the base asset’s price, but their relative price varies with demand (similar to how Augur or Gnosis conditional markets function). **Currency for betting** can be the governance tokens themselves or a stablecoin. A simple design is to use the governance token (bridged) as the currency: users spend their tokens to buy outcome tokens, effectively wagering their governance stake on the decision they think will maximize the metric.
5. **Trading/Betting Phase:** With the market open, participants now **trade outcome tokens** to express their expectations. If Alice believes Proposal A will lead to a better metric outcome, she will buy *A_outcome tokens*, and if she doubts A, she might buy *B_outcome tokens* instead. The **market price of these tokens indicates the collective belief in each proposal’s success**. For example, if an *A_outcome token* is trading at 0.7 times the price of the base token, and *B_outcome* at 0.3, it suggests the market gives Proposal A a 70% chance of being better for the metric. Participants can trade freely during the betting window, adjusting prices based on new information. Solana’s **high throughput and low fees** are crucial here – many micro-transactions and order updates can occur without prohibitive cost, ensuring a lively market. (On Ethereum, such rapid trading for governance would be near-impossible due to gas costs; by contrast, Solana transactions typically cost <$0.01, enabling even smallholders to participate actively.)Throughout trading, **Fission ID’s reputation system can incentivize responsible participation**. Since each trader is tied to an identity, the protocol could monitor behaviors like flip-flopping or dumping large positions last-minute and flag suspicious activity (possibly affecting reputation). Conversely, it might grant *XP points* or badges to identities that provide early liquidity or make prescient trades. This creates a blend of economic and reputational incentives to keep the market honest. Additionally, by having identities, any rewards or airdrops for participation (or compensation if, say, a market is invalidated) can be distributed fairly without duplicate claims.
6. **Market Resolution & Decision:** When the prediction market betting period ends (reaching the decision deadline), the Futarchy governance program determines the “winning” outcome. In a pure futarchy, the decision could be *automatically chosen by the market* – e.g., at closing, if *Proposal A outcome token* price > *Proposal B outcome token* price, then Proposal A is deemed expected to perform better and is **selected for implementation**. Optionally, the protocol might impose a **confidence threshold** or other checks (for example, requiring a certain trading volume or participation from a diverse set of reputations to avoid a thin market deciding critical outcomes). Once the outcome is selected, **the governance decision is officially made on Solana** – e.g., the proposal state is updated to "Approved (via Futarchy)" for Proposal A.Now, to actually reward/punish predictors and finalize governance, there are two phases:Throughout resolution, Fission ID can again come into play by **updating reputations**: users’ Fission ID profiles can record the outcome. For example, Bob’s identity could get a reputation boost tag “Predicted Governance Outcome Correctly – 3 times” if he was on the winning side, whereas a user engaging in malicious manipulation could receive a negative mark. Over many decisions, these identity-linked records build a **reputation ledger of governance participation**, which communities can use to identify reliable contributors or even elect roles (perhaps “oracle committee” members or proposal curators) based on demonstrated skill.
    - **Decision Execution:** The chosen proposal (Proposal A) is executed. If the governance is for a Solana program parameter or treasury action, the Solana **Hub Governance Program** would invoke the necessary instruction (for instance, changing a parameter in a Solana contract, or transferring funds). If the proposal affects external chains (like a protocol upgrade on Ethereum), a cross-chain message via Wormhole can be dispatched. In a hub-spoke model, the **Hub** would send an attested message to the spoke chain instructing it to execute the proposal action. For example, it might call a function on an Ethereum governance executor contract. Wormhole ensures the message is verified by guardians and delivered to the Ethereum contract, which then triggers the needed changes on Ethereum side. In this way, even though the decision was made on Solana, it can *trustlessly propagate* to other chain environments. (If the proposal is only Solana-specific, no external message is needed.)
    - **Outcome Oracle & Market Payout:** At the predetermined future time for evaluating the success metric, an **oracle** reports the metric outcome. This could be a decentralized oracle network or a Wormhole Oracle message if the data comes from off-chain or another chain. The oracle will provide the value of the success metric under the implemented policy (and possibly under the alternative, if needed for comparison). Based on this, the prediction market is **settled**: if Proposal A was enacted, then *A_outcome tokens* pay out one governance token each (or the chosen reward currency), while *B_outcome tokens* expire worthless (or vice versa if B had been enacted). Traders who bet on the correct outcome (the one that was enacted and turned out to be beneficial as per the metric) are rewarded by redeeming their outcome tokens. Those who bet on the losing side lose their stake. This aligns incentives by **rewarding accurate predictions and penalizing incorrect ones**, ideally leading to participants having skin in the game for good governance.
7. **Post-Decision: Cross-Chain Asset and Data Flows:** After governance resolution, users may choose to **withdraw their bridged tokens back to their origin chains**. Using Wormhole, they can burn their wrapped tokens on Solana and unlock the original tokens on (say) Ethereum. The system could optionally support continued staking of governance tokens on Solana if frequent proposals are expected, to avoid constant bridging back-and-forth. Additionally, any **governance records** (like the proposal outcome, votes, prices) can be sent to spoke chains for transparency – e.g., posting an event on Ethereum for record-keeping or so community members on all chains see the results. The identity system might also propagate updates: if a user’s reputation changed due to this proposal, that info could be accessible cross-chain (for instance, a DApp on Polygon could query the user’s Solana Fission ID via Wormhole’s cross-chain queries or by a simple API).

In summary, the architecture comprises:

- **Solana Programs**: (a) *Governance & Futarchy Program* – to manage proposals and prediction market logic; (b) *Identity/Reputation Program (Fission ID)* – to manage NFT identities and store rep scores; (c) standard *Wormhole Bridge contracts* – already deployed on Solana for token and message transfers.
- **Off-chain and Oracles**: Off-chain components like the **Fission ID validator** (AI or other systems to evaluate contributions) and oracle networks to provide the success metric data. These interface with Solana via secure oracles or via Wormhole message posts. For instance, an off-chain agent could send the metric result as a Wormhole message which the Futarchy program consumes to settle markets, thereby avoiding dependence on a single chain’s data.
- **External Chain Contracts**: On Ethereum/Polygon, minimal contracts are needed: typically Wormhole’s standard bridge contracts (already available) and perhaps a **spoke executor contract** to receive governance decisions (if the DAO controls something on that chain). These spoke contracts would trust Wormhole messages from the Solana hub for enacting decisions, similar to how Wormhole MultiGov uses a spoke message executor.

This design emphasizes **modularity and decentralization**: any developer or DAO could adopt it to connect chains. Notably, Wormhole’s relayer-free, guardian-attested model means no centralized entity is needed to carry out cross-chain votes – the infrastructure is generic and **permissionless to extend**. In fact, projects like Uniswap have considered Wormhole for cross-chain governance due to its lightweight deployment and ease of integration into their existing governance process. Our system builds on that foundation, adding Futarchy and identity as the application layer.

### **Solana’s Role and Advantages**

Solana is chosen as the governance hub chain for several key reasons:

- **High Throughput & Low Latency:** Solana’s blockchain can handle thousands of transactions per second with sub-second block times, which is crucial for running **fast-paced prediction markets**. Futarchy relies on market mechanisms – traders may place many orders or adjust positions rapidly as news comes in. Solana’s performance ensures the market can incorporate information quickly, approaching the efficiency of traditional financial exchanges. In contrast, a slower chain could leave markets stale or arbitraged by insiders. High throughput also means the system can support many simultaneous governance markets (multiple proposals) without congestion.
- **Low Transaction Fees:** Transaction fees on Solana average around $0.001–$0.01, a tiny fraction of Ethereum’s fees which often exceed $10. This drastically lowers the barrier for participation – even holders of small amounts of governance tokens can afford to bet in the futarchy market, and can trade in and out multiple times without being eaten by fees. It also means **fine-grained reputation updates** (which might involve on-chain NFT metadata writes) are economically feasible to do frequently. Essentially, Solana makes the micro-transactions of continuous governance viable.
- **Seamless Integration with Fission ID:** Fission ID is implemented on Solana (using NFT and Solana programs), so using Solana as the hub allows the identity system to plug in natively. The governance program can query the Fission ID program synchronously to check a user’s reputation score or verify their linked addresses, all within a single chain’s context. If we tried to do this across chains without a single hub, it would be far more complex (involving cross-chain lookups or off-chain aggregation). Solana serves as the **single source of truth for identity and reputation** in our architecture.
- **Robust Smart Contract Ecosystem:** Solana’s programming model (via Rust-based programs) allows us to build the complex logic required for futarchy markets and identity management. The availability of Openbook market infrastructure or AMM libraries, oracle integrations (Pyth, Switchboard), and Wormhole integration SDKs on Solana accelerates development. For example, an order-book based prediction market could leverage the existing DEX program for matching bets, or an AMM-based market could use a modified Constant Product formula – both approaches are supported by Solana’s ecosystem. Solana programs can also schedule or trigger events (via cranks or bots) which can be used to close markets and invoke oracles at specific slots, automating the governance flow.
- **Security and Proven Infrastructure:** By centralizing the governance logic on Solana, we reduce the attack surface compared to duplicating complex logic on multiple chains. Wormhole’s guardians secure the cross-chain aspects, while Solana’s runtime secures the on-chain execution. Solana has a large validator set and a growing DeFi ecosystem, indicating maturity. Moreover, **Solana’s hub role in MultiGov** (as envisioned by Wormhole Foundation) means it’s a vetted approach for cross-chain governance coordination. Our futarchy model benefits from this established pattern, with Solana providing the reliable backbone.

Finally, Solana’s performance advantages are not just theoretical – they directly impact user experience. A multi-chain token holder can bridge into Solana and **experience governance as fast and user-friendly as a Web2 app**, with near-instant confirmations and negligible costs, something not possible if they stayed on slower base chains. This can drive much higher participation rates and more accurate markets, which are the lifeblood of Futarchy. In essence, Solana enables **“governance at the speed of markets”**.

## 4. Use Case Examples

To illustrate the power and flexibility of this cross-chain Futarchy model, we describe two concrete use cases: one in DeSci and one for a multi-chain DeFi protocol. These examples demonstrate how the system can be applied and the unique benefits of integrating cross-chain stakeholders and identity.

### Use Case 1: DeSci Research Funding Futarchy

**Scenario:** A Decentralized Science DAO operates across Ethereum and Solana, aiming to fund high-impact research projects. The DAO has issued governance tokens on Ethereum (ERC-20) and Solana (SPL) to its members (which include scientists, reviewers, and token investors). They want to decide which of several research proposals to fund using a Futarchy approach – effectively letting the community “bet” on which project will yield the best scientific outcome.

**Process:** Each research proposal (e.g., *Project A: AI-driven cancer research*, *Project B: Open-source climate model*) is turned into a Futarchy governance proposal on Solana. The **success metric** could be something like “number of peer-reviewed publications or citations produced in 2 years” for each project – a metric reflecting research impact. The proposals are set up such that the DAO will fund exactly one project with a grant. All token holders from both Ethereum and Solana can participate by bridging their tokens to Solana via Wormhole (Ethereum holders lock their tokens and receive wrapped tokens on Solana).

A prediction market opens for each pairwise choice (A vs B, A vs C, etc., or structured as multiple markets if many options). Participants – which include domain experts with high Fission ID reputation (e.g., renowned scientists in the DAO), as well as regular token holders – buy outcome tokens corresponding to which project they think will have more impact. Perhaps Alice, an AI researcher, strongly believes Project A will produce more citations than B or C. She purchases outcome tokens favoring A. Bob, a climate scientist, bets on Project B, thinking the climate model will gain more traction.

During the market, **Fission ID ensures that reputable voices are recognized**. Alice’s Fission ID shows she has high expertise (maybe she has an on-chain credential for a PhD and past successful proposals), which might encourage others to trust the market prices she influences. If a brand-new account with no reputation made large bets, others might be cautious or investigate (the identity system brings transparency to who is betting, without necessarily revealing real-world identity, just their on-chain credibility). In this way, **“weighted” information flows into the market** – not by explicitly multiplying anyone’s bets, but by allowing interpretation of market movements with the context of *who* is participating. Additionally, the DAO could impose a rule that a proposal needs at least, say, 3 reputable (above a rep threshold) participants betting in its favor to pass, guarding against a low-quality proposal slipping through with only low-rep bets supporting it.

Once the betting period ends, suppose Project A vs Project B is one decision: the market prices indicate a strong belief that Project A will yield more publications than B. The governance program selects **Project A to fund**. Funds are released (perhaps in USDC) to Project A’s team from the DAO treasury. This execution can happen entirely on Solana if treasury is on Solana, or via Wormhole message to an Ethereum vault if funds are there.

Over the next 2 years, the projects progress and eventually an oracle (or the Fission ID system through its data feeds) updates how many publications each project produced (this could be done by scanning on-chain if publications are recorded, or via a decentralized oracle that counts citations from external data). The success metric is reported: say Project A achieved 5 publications, Project B (which wasn’t funded by the DAO) achieved 3 (perhaps via other funding), and Project C 0. Since Project A was funded and indeed outperformed the alternative, the outcome tokens for A pay out rewards to Alice and others who bet on A. Those who bet on B or C lose their stake (which effectively goes to the winners).

In addition, the DAO can feed this result into Fission ID: Alice and others who accurately forecasted the outcome get a **reputation boost** for contributing to a good funding decision. The researcher team behind Project A might also get a badge on their Fission ID for successfully executing a funded project. Meanwhile, if someone tried to game the system (imagine an entity bet heavily on Project A to make it win, but without good basis – if A had failed to produce results, they would lose their money, discouraging such behavior). Thus, the futarchy mechanism, combined with identity, naturally **penalizes deceptive or spam proposals** and **surfaces genuinely valuable ones**, as people backing bad proposals literally lose money. This aligns with the insight that futarchy provides **market-driven quality control** – overstated claims get filtered out when they don’t deliver.

#### **Benefits in this scenario**

The cross-chain model allowed a wider community to participate (Ethereum and Solana token holders unified), increasing the liquidity and information in the prediction market. Solana’s speed meant even the iterative scientific updates (which might come over months) could be fed in and reacted to by the market if the market remained open for a long period, or in shorter phases. Fission ID ensured that the DAO wasn’t blindly following money – it could see the reputation behind bets, blending expert opinion with crowd wisdom. For DeSci, which values expertise and verifiable credentials, this is crucial. It effectively creates an **open scientific funding tournament**, where ideas compete based on predicted merit, and the community’s skin-in-the-game bets decide winners. The outcome is a potentially more meritocratic and efficient allocation of research funds, with accountability enforced by both financial and reputational stakes.

### Use Case 2: Multi-Chain DeFi Protocol Governance

**Scenario:** A DeFi lending protocol operates a liquidity market on Ethereum and another on Polygon. The protocol’s governance token is present on both chains (users on each chain have some tokens, and they’ve been bridged back and forth at times). The protocol needs to make a governance decision – for example, whether to **increase the interest rate model’s base rate** to improve lender yield. This decision would affect all deployments of the protocol (in fact, both Ethereum and Polygon markets). The team wants a unified decision rather than separate votes on each chain, and they are interested in futarchy to leverage market insights (perhaps anticipating how the rate change might impact total value locked or revenue).

**Process:** The protocol’s governance chooses **Solana as the governance hub** for this futarchy vote (even though previously they did votes on Ethereum via Snapshot, for instance). All governance token holders on Ethereum and Polygon are instructed to **bridge their tokens to Solana via Wormhole** to participate in a special futarchy governance event. Because Wormhole supports both EVM chains, Ethereum users lock their ERC-20 and Polygon users lock their tokens on Polygon, resulting in two wrapped token types on Solana (or, if the token has a common mint, possibly one unified wrapped token – Wormhole can represent each origin, but for governance counting, they might be treated equally). In any case, users from all chains now hold equivalent governance weight on Solana. They also each have (or create) a Fission ID so their identity is known; many participants link their Discord or Github to Fission ID as well, which the team set up earlier for community contributors.

The proposal is “Raise base interest rate from 2% to 4% annually?”. Success metric: “Total protocol liquidity 30 days after implementation” (to see if the rate hike retains or attracts liquidity). This is a binary decision (Yes = raise rate, No = keep it). The Futarchy program on Solana launches a prediction market: *Yes_outcome* vs *No_outcome*. The **currency for betting** could be a stablecoin like USDC that users also bridge, or they can use the governance token itself. Let’s say they use USDC to make interpretation easier (prices directly represent probability if $1 total). The governance tokens are still needed to gauge who can participate or how much, but perhaps the design is that each address can only bet up to the amount of governance tokens they bridged (ensuring token holders are the ones betting and capping influence by stake). Alternatively, the governance token itself is used as collateral.

Now, users across the globe trade on the outcome. Suppose many believe that raising the rate will cause some borrowers to leave, reducing liquidity – thus *Yes_outcome* might initially be priced only ~30%. However, some others think the higher rate will attract more lenders and the effect on borrowers will be minimal, so they start buying *Yes_outcome*, driving it up. Through active trading, the price stabilizes perhaps around 0.55 for Yes vs 0.45 for No, indicating a slight market belief that raising the rate is beneficial. This incorporates inputs like:

- Expert liquidity providers (with high rep) are voicing support and backing it with bets (users can see that known DeFi experts with high Fission ID scores for past contributions are betting “Yes” heavily, which sways sentiment).
- Maybe one whale from Polygon with lots of tokens (but unknown rep) bets “No”, but others counter-bet seeing the fundamentals differently.

At the decision deadline, *Yes_outcome token* is at a higher price, so **Yes (raise rate) is selected**. The Solana governance program now needs to implement this decision on both Ethereum and Polygon markets. Using Wormhole Generic Messaging, it sends an instruction to each chain: essentially, a message to the protocol’s governor contract on Ethereum to call `setBaseRate(0.04)` and similarly on Polygon’s contract. The guardians attest and deliver these messages. On each chain, a small **spoke executor** contract (perhaps a simple Wormhole message receiver that the protocol has deployed) receives the verified message and calls the local contract to update the parameter. Thus, the **base rate is updated on all chains** almost simultaneously, initiated from Solana’s single source of truth. This showcases how **multi-chain protocol changes can be coordinated through a Solana hub**, avoiding divergence.

After 30 days, oracles report the liquidity: let’s say liquidity indeed grew by 10%. The success metric thus validates the decision. The prediction market on Solana resolves: those holding *Yes_outcome* tokens (the winning side) redeem them for payouts (from the losing side’s stakes). Participants like the Polygon whale who bet “No” lose their stake – but this was voluntary risk. More importantly, the DAO gained a lot of insight: the market predicted correctly that liquidity would grow, whereas a simple vote might have been swayed by fear or short-term interest. Those who contributed to this correct prediction (especially if they were informed by expertise) have their **reputation enhanced**. The whale who bet wrong might be a known community member – their Fission ID might get a note of this outcome (not necessarily to punish, but to inform their future credibility).

The outcome is logged on all chains (the Solana program could emit an event that is picked up and posted to an off-chain forum or recorded via the identity NFTs). Users happily bridge their tokens back to Ethereum/Polygon if they want, or keep some on Solana anticipating future governance rounds. The protocol plans to use this futarchy mechanism for other big decisions, now that they’ve seen cross-chain governance can work efficiently.

#### **Benefits in this scenario**

In this multi-chain scenario, futarchy prevented a split-brain governance (where Ethereum holders might vote one way and Polygon another). It also provided a **financially driven forecast** of the impact of the decision, which proved accurate and likely led to a better outcome than either an emotional community vote or a team mandate. Wormhole allowed this to happen without launching a new token or requiring everyone to migrate to one chain permanently – it was an **ad hoc unified assembly** of all token holders on a fast chain. Solana’s speed meant the market had sufficient activity and the decision was reached in, say, a few days rather than a lengthy off-chain vote process. Identity via Fission ID gave the process a layer of trust: for example, if there was a sudden surge of bets from newly created wallets, the team could notice the lack of Fission IDs or low reputation and alert participants to be cautious or extend the market for more information. In effect, the system is **resilient and informed**.

This demonstrates how even complex multi-chain governance (a historically tricky problem, as seen with protocols like Uniswap deploying on multiple L2s) can be handled with a hub-and-spoke futarchy model. The **Solana hub coordinates all chains’ interests**, ensuring one consistent decision, and the **Wormhole network carries the inputs and outputs** (tokens in, decisions out) trustlessly. The Fission ID ensures it's not just blind capital flows but a community of recognized contributors making the decision.

## 5. Conclusion

The proposed cross-chain Futarchy governance model brings together **prediction market-based decision making**, **cross-chain token participation**, and **decentralized identity** to create a governance system that is both inclusive and meritocratic. By using Wormhole, token holders from any chain can partake in a unified Solana-based governance process, leveraging Solana’s performance to achieve near real-time, low-cost collective decision-making. Fission ID’s identity and reputation layer adds a human dimension to this system – aligning on-chain actions with persistent identities and track records, which mitigates the pitfalls of purely anonymous token voting and fosters a culture of accountable participation.

Technically, the solution is feasible with today’s technology:

- Wormhole provides the **bridging and messaging** infrastructure out-of-the-box, already supporting Ethereum, Polygon, and Solana connectivity for both tokens and arbitrary instructions.
- Solana’s smart contract ecosystem can implement the **Futarchy logic and NFT identity system**, as evidenced by existing Solana DAO frameworks and Fission ID’s own implementation plans.
- The **hub-and-spoke governance architecture** is in line with Wormhole’s MultiGov design, which is being actively developed and adopted. This means our model can integrate with emerging standards for cross-chain DAO governance rather than being a standalone silo.

In terms of innovation, this model demonstrates:

- **Cross-Chain Futarchy:** moving beyond single-chain futarchy experiments by aggregating a larger, more diverse set of participants across ecosystems, which should improve market quality and decision confidence.
- **Reputation-augmented governance:** introducing a feedback loop where good governance behavior (like accurate predictions or valuable contributions) is recorded and rewarded via identity, potentially creating better long-term governance outcomes (as experienced members gain influence and irresponsible actors are discouraged).
- **Solana as a Governance Hub:** showcasing Solana’s strengths not just for DeFi or NFTs, but as a *governance backbone* that can handle the load of an entire multi-chain community’s decision process in one place. This could position Solana as a go-to network for decentralized governance services.
